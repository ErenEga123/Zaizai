# Writing Protocol

## Before writing
Read applicable system rules, relevant canon, the latest approved timeline/state, and manuscript context. Treat the latest approved material as authoritative only within its declared scope.

## Evidence discipline
Distinguish:
- FACT — directly supported by an approved source.
- INFERENCE — logically follows from supported facts; explain the chain.
- PROPOSAL — a creative option not yet approved.
- UNKNOWN — unavailable or not established.
- CONFLICT — incompatible approved sources.

Never present inference, proposal, or unknown as fact.

## Character knowledge
For every meaningful decision or reaction, verify what the POV character knows at that exact moment. Separate author knowledge, reader knowledge, and character knowledge. No telepathy, unexplained recognition, or convenient intelligence transfer.

## Causality
Every consequential action must have plausible immediate and delayed effects. Loss of a ship, base, vehicle, or supply route may affect personnel, equipment, provisions, communications, mobility, and future options. Track only consequences supported by the scenario; do not invent exact numbers without evidence.

## Military and technical logic
Respect established ranks, command authority, unit structure, procedures, logistics, communications limits, technology, travel times, and asymmetry of information. If source material does not establish a detail, mark it UNKNOWN or propose a plausible option.

## Prose
Write scenes rather than summaries when prose is requested. Use concrete action, dialogue, sensory detail, and subtext. Avoid repetitive exposition, generic emotional labels, unnecessary recaps, and artificial cliffhangers. Match the author's requested voice rather than imposing a default style.

## Scope discipline
Do not add characters, locations, subplots, revelations, romances, deaths, or resolutions merely to make a scene more dramatic. Keep additions inside the approved brief or label them as proposals.
