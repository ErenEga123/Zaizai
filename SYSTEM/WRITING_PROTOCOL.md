# Writing protocol

## Before prose
Determine mode, POV, tense, scene objective, emotional turn, and length. If unspecified, infer conservative defaults from adjacent approved chapters and mention the choice only when it materially matters.

A scene brief captures where and when the scene occurs; POV and perception limits; participants and motives; entry conditions; intended change; required beats and forbidden outcomes; continuity dependencies.

## Prose rules
- Write scenes, not summaries, unless summary is requested.
- Use concrete action, dialogue, sensory detail, and meaningful subtext.
- Give each character a distinct voice grounded in their dossier and current state.
- Let characters act on their own goals and incomplete information.
- Respect travel time, communication delay, physical limits, chain of command, resources, injuries, and consequences.
- Do not make characters conveniently omniscient.
- Do not resolve an open thread merely because the scene would be neater.
- Avoid repetitive exposition, recap dialogue, generic emotional labels, and ornamental filler.
- Do not repeat the same beat in different wording.
- Preserve established names, ranks, titles, terminology, and spelling.
- Do not establish major characters, factions, technology, powers, locations, or historical facts without author approval.

## Canon labels
- **CANON:** explicitly author-approved.
- **DERIVED:** unavoidable consequence of canon; cite premises.
- **PROPOSAL:** creative option awaiting approval.
- **UNKNOWN:** not established in available records.
- **CONFLICT:** two sources disagree.

Never promote DERIVED, PROPOSAL, or UNKNOWN to CANON without author approval.

## Knowledge boundaries
For each POV and speaking character, check what they know at the exact scene time. Separate direct observation, reported information, inference, suspicion, and false belief. A character may infer incorrectly; narration must not present their inference as objective fact unless the POV permits it.

## Length and completeness
Honor requested scope. If asked for a complete chapter, produce a complete chapter with a beginning, development, turn, and ending—not an outline or teaser. Do not stop early merely to offer continuation.
